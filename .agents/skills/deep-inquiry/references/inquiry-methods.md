# Inquiry methods: escape the familiar-answer loop

Read before first convergence on a substantive open-ended task, or when new evidence shows a framing failure. Use the smallest relevant set of moves. No fixed lens count, mandatory contrarian answer or novelty score applies.

## Detect a framework that is substituting for an answer

Look for observable symptoms: candidates differ only in names; a framework is proposed without a working mechanism; finer metrics never resolve the same attribution ambiguity; each repair requires more manual administration; recommendations assume cooperation or powers nobody has; a proposed experiment cannot change the decision; or repeated critique changes wording but reveals nothing.

Treat these as prompts to inspect a premise, not proof that the conventional answer is wrong. Identify the familiar answer's strongest useful part and the exact condition it depends on. The aim is not to access hidden pretraining probabilities or generate unlikely text; it is to produce substantively different, testable mechanisms.

## Challenge the required intermediate step

Trace objective <- decision <- supposedly required information/activity. Ask whether the intermediate step is necessary, observable, identifiable under the available assumptions, and economical to obtain. Name which claim is supported and which is unverified.

Ask how the same underlying decision is made without the new technology or disputed metric. Use the analogue to question an assumption, not as proof. Search for an authorized mechanism that reaches the confirmed objective without the questionable prerequisite. If this exposes materially different interpretations of the user's problem, return to Q before adopting one.

Do not jump from "hard to measure" to "do not measure." Retain observable costs, outputs, safety checks and contractual requirements where relevant. Test the opposite case: when would precise measurement be cheap, identifiable and actually necessary? A good method must allow the familiar solution to win there.

## Change the mechanism, not the vocabulary

| Move | Question | Concrete output and guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Remove a prerequisite | Can the confirmed outcome be achieved without this activity or exact intermediate quantity? | A bypass mechanism and retained constraints; no silent deletion of mandatory deliverables. |
| Reverse sequence or direction | Can prevention replace repair, exceptions replace blanket processing, or demand replace supply-driven work? | A changed flow plus the cases where reversal fails. |
| Change unit or level | Is the useful unit a task, shared workflow, team, portfolio or decision event? | Different intervention and observables; confirm changed problem interpretation through Q. |
| Separate a false tradeoff | Are different populations, times or risks forced into one rule? | Separate rules by justified boundary, with interface and exception handling. |
| Apply a limiting case | What breaks at zero extra staff, much higher load or perfect information? | A structural dependency or bound; restore real constraints before adoption. |
| Borrow a structural analogue | How is the same uncertainty or coordination problem handled in another domain? | Map relationships and explicitly identify where the analogy fails. |
| Change representation | What appears in a formula, state transition, concrete transaction or dependency sketch that prose concealed? | A checked bound, missing transition or counterexample, not decorative diagrams. |
| Model adaptation | Who rationally avoids, games or shifts costs under this mechanism? | A revised incentive, control or support rule, not imagined stakeholder agreement. |

For each useful candidate, name the changed premise/mechanism, what remains fixed, necessary conditions, and a disconfirming observation. Do not force a matrix of every move against every task.

## Expand briefly, then converge fairly

Before polishing a preferred draft, sketch mechanism-distinct concepts at comparable detail. Include a viable simple/conventional approach when relevant. Do not reject a promising early concept only because it is less polished, or accept it merely for novelty. Restore hard constraints before selection. Avoid blending incompatible mechanisms into a superficially attractive hybrid.

Challenge the preferred approach with a concrete counterexample, missing decisive fact, better mechanism under the same constraints, or a condition that reverses the choice. The challenger must support the challenge. Rebut unsupported criticism instead of degrading a sound answer. An independently generated concept can reduce reliance on the first draft only when its inputs really exclude that draft; same-session roleplay is not independent evidence.

## Make it work on an ordinary day

Walk one actual or clearly synthetic transaction: trigger -> owner/authority -> available input -> action -> handoff/exception -> observable result. Count new reporting, logging, review, coordination and maintenance work as part of the mechanism's cost. Do not prescribe a laboratory setup that the environment has already ruled out.

Then consider what happens after participants adapt and after the system scales. Does the bottleneck move? Does the metric improve while the objective worsens? Who gains authority, who bears risk and who pays transition costs? Test plausible positive and negative responses as assumptions, not measured preferences.

For a requested business offering, go beyond a principle: after a customer understands the idea, what concrete decision, configuration, contract, implementation or recurring service remains to be delivered? Apply this only when commercial delivery is part of the task, not to every problem.

## Keep discoveries distinct from defects

Record only consequential discoveries: a premise invalidated, a new mechanism, a discriminating observation, an action-changing bound, or an important remaining uncertainty. Label each as fact with evidence, hypothesis, or assumption-based simulation, and state what action changes. A discovery log is not a private reasoning transcript.

If another pass would only rewrite the same answer, obtain the missing evidence, ask the user-owned question or stop. Initial concept exploration is not revision; reopening a selected core mechanism follows the root's re-exploration policy. Exploration is not a loophole for unlimited candidates or uncounted rewrites.

## Contrasting synthetic examples

**Unnecessary attribution:** a team must select a resource mix, but individual contributions cannot be distinguished from its aggregate records. Consider a decision rule based on observable cost, outcomes and accountable choices only if this matches the confirmed objective. Test measurement-required cases and incentive failure too. Do not generalize the conclusion to all collaborative work.

**A familiar calculation is sufficient:** isolated identical jobs change from twelve to three seconds. For one hundred sequential jobs, saved processing time is nine hundred seconds. Calculate it; do not invent a governance problem or claim business-level causal ROI.

**A hidden bound:** drafting takes twenty minutes in a two-hundred-minute sequential workflow. Saving sixteen drafting minutes reduces total time by eight percent under unchanged waiting/rework. Even eliminating drafting saves at most ten percent. This challenges a drafting-only strategy without proving the cause of waiting.
