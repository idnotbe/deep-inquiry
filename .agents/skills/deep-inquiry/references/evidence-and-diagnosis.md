# Evidence and causal diagnosis

Read for E/H or before proposing an experiment. Produce a decision-relevant evidence update, not a catalog of facts.

## Evidence that changes action

Specify the unknown claim, the decision it affects, what observation could distinguish the possibilities, and the least burdensome authorized source or test. Check supplied materials and primary sources; verify current capabilities and mutable facts rather than relying on memory. Respect requests restricting external research. For an unfamiliar or recently changing model, product or API name, verify the exact spelling in an available current primary source before correcting it or declaring it nonexistent. Low effort or familiarity is not evidence. If lookup is unavailable, preserve the exact name and mark the claim unverified.

Ask: would any plausible result change the next action? If not, the check may be unnecessary for this decision. Include collection, instrumentation, duplicate-work, delay, disruption, privacy and review costs. Do not claim a precise value-of-information calculation without suitable inputs. Never interpret "measurement costs too much" as permission to waive required correctness or mandatory evidence.

Use source comparison for quotations/translations, calculation for arithmetic, logs and executable tests for behavior, and authorized experiments where causal evidence requires them. Distinguish a plan to collect evidence from the actual evidence collected. Preserve provenance, assumptions, units and relevant time/context. Paraphrase source explanations; mark brief verbatim quotations explicitly and cite their actual source. Never manufacture a citation or present copied prose as your own synthesis.

Classify evidence as established within scope, contradicted, or unverified. If a decisive premise is false, route to premise-collapse handling. If materials or permission are required, use Q. If unavailable evidence is necessary for current acceptance, stop with that requirement unmet or ask about scope. If the fact is inherently future-dependent, use future-uncertainty handling rather than endless present-day searches.

## Distinguish causes from remedies

A causal hypothesis explains why an event occurred; a solution alternative describes what to do. Form two or three plausible causal hypotheses where diagnosis is needed, then specify observations that would differ under each. Acquire the most discriminating permissible evidence through E. Plausibility, temporal coincidence and familiar labels do not establish a cause.

Represent the mechanism as intervention -> behavior/system change -> intermediate result -> objective. Identify the fragile link and what would support or refute it. Consider interactions and confounders before attributing an outcome to one component. Classify hypotheses as supported, ruled out or unverified; act when the distinction is sufficient for the decision, not when every possible cause is exhausted.

## Missing data versus non-identifiability

Missing data means potentially useful observations have not been obtained. Non-identifiability means the available observations and assumptions can support different underlying explanations with different answers. Show the ambiguity and scope before using that label; difficulty, mixed contributors or an unsuccessful search alone does not prove impossibility.

A synthetic example: two unobserved contribution assignments yield the same aggregate output, so that aggregate alone does not select an individual contribution. Additional design, assumptions or data might distinguish them. Do not generalize this to all individual contributions, all AI tasks or all ROI measurement.

If precise attribution is not required by the confirmed objective, investigate mechanisms that can make a sound decision using observable quantities and explicit uncertainty. If attribution itself is mandatory, preserve that requirement and disclose what is missing. Do not replace it silently with a convenient management framework.

## Research beyond the initial vocabulary

When a verified limitation exposes a framing weakness, search for the underlying structure as well as the original topic. Seek disconfirming cases and non-domain analogues, not only sources supporting the draft. Explain which relation transfers and which conditions do not. Treat remembered examples and hypothetical analogies as hypotheses until verified. A known open or long-unsolved problem is not, by itself, a reason to abandon an attempt; report the support and limits of the actual result.
